## 2. The Psychology: Why This Nut Has Been Hard to Crack

You've hit the real reason nobody has data on *how* families pay: **parents don't talk about it, often not even with each other.** That isn't an accident. Several well-known psychological forces keep it quiet:

| What's going on | What it sounds like | What it means for marketing |
|---|---|---|
| **It's love, not a line item.** Spending on a kid's sport feels like an investment in *them*, not an expense. | "I'll do whatever it takes for her." | **Never tell them to spend less.** That attacks their love. Honor it. |
| **Money shame and secrecy.** Debt is private; sports is public. | "We're fine." (The card isn't.) | Make it **safe and anonymous**. The survey is the first place they've ever been asked. |
| **Keeping up with the team.** Nobody wants their kid to be the one who can't go to the showcase. | "Everyone else is going." | **Show them they're not alone**: "[X]% of us carry a sports card balance" (your real survey number). The aggregate data removes the shame. |
| **The scholarship dream.** The spending feels like it will "pay back." | "It'll pay for college." | Don't kill the dream. **Add a backup plan**: "Fund the dream so it pays you back either way." |
| **Sunk cost.** "We've already put $40K in, we can't stop now." | "We're in too deep." | Speak to the *next* dollar, not the last: "From here on, every dollar can work twice." |
| **Spouse tension.** One parent spends, the other worries. | Arguments after tournaments. | Give couples a shared plan and language. That's a huge, hidden pain point. |

**The marketing principle that comes out of all this:**
> **"You're not going to stop paying. We're not asking you to. We're going to show you how to pay for it smarter, and how to get it back."**

That line is the one nobody in youth sports is saying. Every other voice either sells *more* sports (clubs, camps) or tells parents to *cut back* (financial media). BWB is the only one saying **keep playing, fund it smarter**.

## 7. The "$10,000 for 10 Years" Video (Truth Concepts or Carrier Illustration)

**What it is:** Truth Concepts is the calculator software widely used by infinite banking practitioners (built by Todd Langford) to model policy cash value, loans and repayment over time. You're right that it models the *strategy* well, while the Vault handles the *debt-blasting output*.

**Important:** I shouldn't invent the numbers. A real "$10,000 a year, borrow, repay, repeat" picture depends on the specific carrier, policy design, dividend rates and loan terms, and whole life illustrations show a **guaranteed** column and a **projected, non-guaranteed** column. The numbers must come from **a real illustration run by a licensed agent** (Chris, Nick or Craig).

**Storyboard (about 2.5 min):**
1. **Hook (0:00–0:15):** "Travel ball costs this family $10,000 a year. Watch what happens when that money works twice."
2. **Path A, the normal way (0:15–0:45):** $10K goes out every year for 10 years. Counter: **$100,000 spent, $0 left.**
3. **Path B, the recapture way (0:45–1:45):** $10K a year goes into the policy, the family borrows against it to pay for the season and repays the loan. Animated two-column chart (guaranteed vs. projected) using **the real illustration numbers**.
4. **The reveal (1:45–2:15):** year-10 comparison: cash value, death benefit, and what's available for the *next* kid or college.
5. **Close (2:15–2:30):** "Every family's numbers are different. Find your Sports Money DNA and see your version." Required disclosures on screen.

**What I need from the agents:** one sample illustration (PDF or Truth Concepts export) for a $10K-a-year design, plus their compliance team's approved disclaimer text. With that, I can build the animated chart.
