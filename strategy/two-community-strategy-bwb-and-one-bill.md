# Ball Without Boundaries + ONE BILL: How the Two Communities Work Together

*Strategy memo, November 7, 2026.*

---

## The Short Answer

**Yes, two communities, but launch them in sequence, not at the same time.**

- **Ball Without Boundaries (BWB)** on **Circle**: the **niche**. Sports parents. Paid premium, with free founding members.
- **ONE BILL** on **Mighty Networks**: the **mission**. Every American. Free front door with a paid Inner Circle.

**Launch BWB first and make ONE BILL the method you teach inside it.** Once BWB is humming, spin ONE BILL out as its own free community for everyone.

This is exactly Scott MacGregor's path. He started niche (the Talent Champions Council, for talent leaders), proved it, then broadened into The Outlier Project. Every successful community in the research started **narrow** and widened later.

---

## Why Not Launch Both at Once

| Risk | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Founder burnout** | Every sticky community in the research had a founder showing up daily. Two communities means two daily rhythms, two welcome flows and two event calendars. |
| **Split attention, split proof** | 50 engaged members in one place beats 25 in two. Density creates the "this place is alive" feeling. |
| **Two platforms, two bills, two logins** | Members on both have to juggle accounts. That's friction, the thing MacGregor removed first. |
| **Unclear story** | People need to know *which door is theirs*. Sequence makes the story obvious. |

**Exception:** if you have a partner or community manager who can own one of the two daily, running them side by side becomes realistic.

## Phased Plan

### Phase 1 (Months 0–3): BWB launches, and ONE BILL lives inside it
- Hand-pick **25–50 founding sports parents** (free for life, in writing).
- **Run the Sports Family Money DNA survey** as their first act: "Help us find out the truth about how families really pay for youth sports."
- Inside BWB, run a monthly **"ONE BILL: The Sports Bill"** challenge. This is your test kitchen for the ONE BILL method.
- Goal: 50–100 engaged members, plus survey data from at least 200 families (founders share it with their teams).

### Phase 2 (Months 3–6): Publish the data and prove the method
- Publish **"The Real Cost of the Game: How Sports Families Actually Pay"**, an anonymous findings report. It answers the question nobody else answers (see the "a third go into debt" section below).
- Pitch it to media, sports podcasts, club directors and Project Play itself. **Original data is the strongest free marketing there is.**
- Collect ONE BILL wins from BWB members: "Killed our $340/month tournament-travel credit card balance."
- Open BWB to paid members (founders stay free).

### Phase 3 (Month 6+): ONE BILL launches on Mighty Networks
- Launch the **free ONE BILL community** with the BWB success stories as proof.
- Everyone from BWB gets a **ONE BILL Inner Circle founding seat, free**. That's a thank-you that costs you nothing and seeds the new community with engaged people on day one.
- From here, the referral loop runs both ways.

## The Big Insight: Nobody Knows What "Going Into Debt" Means

You're right: the research tracks **how much** families spend, and almost nothing about **how** they pay.

| Source | What it says | What it *doesn't* say |
|---|---|---|
| **Aspen Project Play (2024)** | Average $1,016 per child on the primary sport, **up 46% since 2019**; parents spend $40B+ a year; estimates range from $0 to almost $25,000 | How it's paid for |
| **Good Sports / Harris Poll** | **18% took on debt**; 23% took on extra work | What *kind* of debt |
| **New York Life (2025)** | **12% went into debt** | What kind, how much, or for how long |
| **LendingTree (2021)** | **79% have been in debt** for kids' competitive activities (all activities, not just sports) | Sports specifically |
| **LendingTree (2022)** | **59% say fall sports are a financial strain** | — |
| **TD Ameritrade / Harris (2019)** | 1 in 3 took fewer vacations; **1 in 5 got second jobs or delayed retirement** | — |

**Debt estimates swing from 12% to 79%** because every survey defines "debt" differently, and **nobody breaks it down** into credit cards vs. HELOCs vs. refinancing vs. 401(k) loans vs. buy-now-pay-later vs. club payment plans. I couldn't find any public data on HELOC or retirement-account use for youth sports.

**That gap is BWB's opportunity.** Whoever publishes the first real breakdown of *how* sports families pay becomes the go-to source. Every reporter writing about youth sports costs will quote it. That could be Ball Without Boundaries.

And your other insight is the heart of it: **families aren't going to stop paying.** So the question isn't "should you spend less on sports?" (that's a losing message with sports parents). It's "**how do you pay for it without wrecking everything else?**" That's a ONE BILL question.

## ONE BILL on Mighty Networks: More Thinking

**Positioning:** "Take control of your money, ONE bill at a time." No budgets to fail at, no shame, just one bill.

**Structure:**
- **Free community** (the mission): weekly "What's your ONE bill?" prompts, wins feed, a community "Bills Killed" counter, beginner guides.
- **Paid Inner Circle**: the monthly Kill-a-Bill challenge with live coaching, accountability buddies, expert Q&As (insurance, phone and utility negotiators, credit counselors), and templates and scripts ("how to call your provider").
- **Topic spaces by bill type:** Housing · Car · Insurance · Phone and Internet · Subscriptions · Credit Cards · **Kids' Activities** (the bridge to BWB) · Medical.

**The ONE BILL member journey:**
1. **Join free** and answer "What's your ONE bill?"
2. **Get matched** to the space and the monthly challenge for that bill.
3. **Kill it** (lower it, refinance it or pay it off) and post the win.
4. **The win adds to the community counter**, gets recognition, and earns an invite to "pick your next ONE bill."
5. **Upgrade** to the Inner Circle for coaching on the harder bills.

**Hospitality layer:** a personal welcome, a buddy by day 7, a check-in if someone goes quiet for 14 days, and a celebration for every bill killed.

---

## A Word on Trust and Compliance

You'll be collecting sensitive financial information, so the experience has to feel safe.
- Let people **answer anonymously**, and use **ranges** instead of exact dollar amounts.
- Say plainly: "**We will never sell your data.** Results are only shared in aggregate."
- Keep ONE BILL in **education and community, not individualized financial advice**, unless you have licensed professionals involved. Have a lawyer glance at the privacy notice and disclaimers before launch.
