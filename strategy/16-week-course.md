## The Short Answer to "Can We Do an Hour of Mindset?"

**Yes, but not an hour of talking.** Your book is already built the right way: every chapter is **mindset first** ("Reason"), then a **sports story** ("On the Court"), then **one action** ("One Thing to Try"). That's a proven teaching rhythm. We turn each chapter into a 60-minute live session where the mindset is the hook and the money move is what they leave with.

People stay for an hour when they **talk, laugh, win and commit**, not when they listen. So each hour is split like a practice, not a lecture.

---

## The 60-Minute "Practice" Format (Same Every Week)

| Time | Segment | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:10 | **Warm-Up: Wins & Reps** | Members share last week's "One Thing to Try." Celebrate every win, big or small. Update the community "Bills Killed" counter. |
| 0:10–0:25 | **Film Session: The Mindset** | Tom teaches the week's Reason from the book. Tell the "On the Court" story. One big idea, no slides needed. |
| 0:25–0:40 | **The Play: The Money Move** | One practical money skill tied to that mindset (a worksheet, a number to find, a call to make). This is where they "drop a couple of things." |
| 0:40–0:55 | **Scrimmage: Breakouts or Hot Seat** | Small groups of 3–4 (Bill Buddies) work the play together, or one family volunteers for a live "hot seat" walkthrough. |
| 0:55–1:00 | **Huddle: Commit** | Everyone types their "One Thing to Try" in the chat. That's next week's Warm-Up. |

**Why this works:** the mindset opens the door, the money move gives them a win, and the commitment brings them back next week. The research on sticky communities (rituals, visible progress, member-to-member connection) all lives inside this format.

## Before the Book Becomes Course Material: Four Fixes

Your book is strong. A few lines should be checked before it's taught as a course, because the course will be recorded and repeated:

1. **"$200 a month at 25 grows to over $500,000; at 45, about $90,000."** The math depends on the assumed return (roughly 7% gets you about $525,000 from 25 to 65; the "at 45" number fits closer to 6%). Use one stated rate for both, for example "assuming 7% a year, not guaranteed."
2. **"Odds of a Division I scholarship from club volleyball are under 2%."** Add a source (for example, the NCAA's published odds), or soften to "very small."
3. **Reason 5: "borrow from it — your own money — without asking a bank," and "It doesn't crash with the market."** The Ostrow plan's compliance gates say a policy loan comes from the insurer, accrues interest and can reduce cash value and death benefit. Have Chris Naugle's team's compliance review this paragraph so the course matches what licensed agents can say.
4. **Small detail:** the cover says "EST. 2026" and the footer says "© 2025." Pick one.
