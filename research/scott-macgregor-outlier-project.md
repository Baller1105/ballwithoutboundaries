# Deep Dive: Scott MacGregor, SomethingNew LLC & The Outlier Project

*Research compiled November 7, 2026. Note: his name is spelled **MacGregor** (not McGregor). Searching "McGregor" turns up the wrong people.*

---

## TL;DR: The Playbook in One Paragraph

Scott MacGregor did **not** start with a cold, paid community. He spent about 20 years building a personal network as a sales leader. He started a recruiting firm (SomethingNew, 2015) and published four "gratitude" anthology books that each brought in dozens of named contributors. In about 2020–21 he launched a **niche, business-adjacent community** for talent and HR leaders, the **Talent Champions Council**. He then **rebranded and broadened it into The Outlier Project (TOP)**, dropped the application form because it added friction, and sold **cheap annual memberships with a lifetime price lock** (around $480/yr, rising to $600). Growth came almost entirely from **word of mouth, public LinkedIn welcome posts for every new member, and a heavy podcast-guest circuit**, all fed by a large calendar of live events. I found **no evidence of paid advertising**. By 2024–25 TOP reported **750+ members in 30–35 countries**. He now sells **higher-priced tiers** ($249/month "invite-only" tier), a **$1,500 cohort program** (free for members), retreats, and a **magazine**.

## 2. SomethingNew LLC (2015 →): The "HR" Company

- **What it is:** an executive search and talent strategy firm (recruiting, mainly sales and client-success roles), based in **Madison, CT**, with 11–50 employees. Founded **2015**.
- **Brand moves he used, which carried over into the community:**
  - Won the American Business Award for Innovation **6–8 times**, depending on the source. He publicizes awards heavily.
  - Publishes a **Net Promoter Score of 94.4** on the team page.
  - **SomethingGood™**: a social-mission arm ("good companies do good things").
  - **"National TrySomethingNew Week" (July 9–15)**: a holiday he made up himself as a PR stunt, a cheap way to earn attention.
  - **The Ripple Report**: an annual impact report (2019 PDF on the firm's site).
  - **Forbes Councils** member profile.
- **Philosophy:** "People Over Everything" (#peopleovereverything), which became the community's hashtag DNA.

## 3. The Standing O! Book Series: The Hidden Network Engine

- Four books: *Standing O!*, *Encore*, *Salute*, *Tribute*. Each is a **collection of gratitude essays from athletes, CEOs and entrepreneurs** about people who shaped them. Forewords come from Dick Vermeil (former NFL coach) and Heather Monahan.
- **Why this is genius for community-building:** every contributor becomes an invested co-promoter and a warm relationship. Four books with dozens of contributors each means **hundreds of high-profile people already "in his circle"** before the community launched. Treat this as a pre-launch audience-builder, not just a book.

## 4. Phase 1: Talent Champions Council (about 2020–2022)

- **His own origin story** (podcast interviews): he had "a very strong desire to start a community," but because he ran a recruiting firm, he **didn't want to alienate his SomethingNew employees** or confuse the business. So he started a community "centered around all things talent": the **Talent Champions Council (TCC)**.
- **Positioning:** "The leading private membership community for innovative Talent Strategy … for anyone who believes people are a company's greatest asset." Members were talent acquisition, onboarding and retention leaders from **corporate, military and pro sports** backgrounds.
- **Dates:** Crunchbase lists a 2020 founding with co-founders (Christine D. W.; Nicole Smartt Serres in one listing). One member lists himself as a **"Founding Member, Mar 2021."** One 2021 podcast called it a non-profit, while Crunchbase lists it as for-profit.
- **Strategic read:** the niche was **adjacent to his paying business**. Every TCC member was also a potential SomethingNew client or referral source, which made the community a lead-generation asset from day one.
- **Free or paid?** I could not confirm whether TCC was free. The evidence points to a low-cost or "council" style membership. The first prices on record are under the TOP name (see below).

## 5. Phase 2: The Rebrand into The Outlier Project (about 2022–2023)

- **Why "Outlier":** "I have always identified as an outlier, and the people I wanted to give exposure to are outliers too." The tagline is **"Anyone can be ordinary"**, and you choose to be extraordinary.
- **Key strategic shifts:**
  1. **Broadened the niche.** It went from talent and HR only to "the most eclectic community I can think of. It's not constrained to an industry or job title."
  2. **Killed the application.** He planned an application process but decided it "was going to be friction," so he opened membership: "let me see what happens when we open up membership and anybody can join."
  3. **Low entry price with a lifetime lock.** A LinkedIn post (around August 2023) offered existing members a lifetime lock at **$480/yr** and said **new members pay $600 starting Sept 1**. "Joining takes 30 seconds." That is a deadline-driven urgency campaign.
  4. **Scholarship pricing.** The "TOP Scholarship" offered recent graduates a **full annual membership for $99**, a pipeline of young members and goodwill.
- **Early members on record:** Mike Perlow (Perlow Productions) has been a member since **December 2022**.

## 7. What Members Get (the Retention Engine)

- **200+ live, interactive virtual events per year.** That is roughly four a week and the core of the value.
- **"Legend Series":** live conversations with well-known founders. The guests promote the session to their own audiences.
- **Local, member-initiated meetups** in the US and internationally.
- **Retreats**: "Retreat No. 01" (4 days) and a Rhode Island retreat.
- **Humanity Expeditions** (experience and service trips) and **invitation-only retreats**.
- **Monthly community challenges** and a **weekly recap newsletter**.
- **TOP Advisors**: a roster of member experts (MacGregor covers entrepreneurship, relationships, sales and mindset).
- **Identity items**: membership card, merch, Hall of Fame.
- **Founder touch**: a member testimonial says "while there are hundreds of people in the community, Scott takes the time to know each individual."

## 10. The Pricing Evolution (Value Ladder)

1. **$99/yr**: scholarship for new grads
2. **$480/yr**: early-member lifetime lock
3. **$600/yr**: standard annual (late 2023)
4. **$249/month (~$3,000/yr)**: current "TOP" invite-only tier
5. **$1,500**: "Who's Your Oprah" cohort (bundled free with membership)
6. Retreats, expeditions and the magazine on top

The pattern: **open the doors cheap and frictionless, build density and evangelists, then raise prices and add exclusivity ("invite-only") once demand exists.**

## 11. Applying This to ONE BILL: The MacGregor Playbook

1. **Start with your existing network, not strangers.** His first members were 20 years of relationships plus book contributors. Do a "Standing O!" equivalent: a **ONE BILL story anthology** (for example, "The Bill That Changed My Life") with 30–50 contributors. Each one becomes a founding member and promoter.
2. **Launch niche and adjacent, then broaden.** TCC was talent-only, next to his business. Start ONE BILL with a tight niche (for example, dual-income families with debt, or financial coaches), then widen.
3. **Remove friction early.** No application. "Joining takes 30 seconds."
4. **Founding-member price with a lifetime lock and a hard deadline.** For example, $X/yr locked forever, rising on a named date.
5. **Welcome every member publicly on LinkedIn and Instagram.** This is the single most copyable growth tactic: free, personal, and each post reaches a new network.
6. **Make events the product.** Several live sessions a week, a "Legends"-style guest series, local meetups, then an annual retreat.
7. **Go on 2–4 podcasts a month.** Pitch the *philosophy* (his was "the outlier mindset"; yours is **financial awareness through ONE BILL**), not the product.
8. **Create a price anchor.** A $1,500 cohort that is "free when you join" makes membership a no-brainer.
9. **Raise prices and add exclusivity once you have density.** Start at $99–$480 and grow toward an invite-only premium tier.
10. **Platform:** start lean (Circle is where he ended up). Don't over-build before you have 100 people.

**The customer-experience parallel:** MacGregor's edge was "Ritz-Carlton" treatment at community scale: knowing each member, public recognition, membership cards, Hall of Fame. That is the hospitality layer your community should have.
