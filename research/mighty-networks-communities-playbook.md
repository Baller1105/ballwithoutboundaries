## Part 2: What They All Have in Common

1. **An audience first, community second** (same as Circle). A podcast (Wealth Without Wall Street), a brand (Oiselle, PLANTSTRONG), an author or teacher (WealthBuilders). Nobody started cold.
2. **They left Facebook Groups.** Wealth Without Wall Street, PLANTSTRONG and others moved because Facebook gave them no ownership, no payments and too much noise. On their own platform, "members come on purpose."
3. **One home for everything.** Courses, events, community and payments in a single place, ideally a **branded mobile app** people keep on their phone.
4. **Freemium is common on Mighty.** Unlike Circle's mostly paid-only examples, the biggest Mighty communities often run a **free community as the front door** with **paid courses or a paid inner circle**.
5. **The content engine feeds the community.** A podcast or show creates the reason to come back (the weekly guest Q&A).
6. **They sell the result, not the access.** Finance communities lead with outcomes: "3,000+ investment accounts opened," "$8.2M invested."
7. **Scarcity and timing.** Oiselle opens twice a year, and Versed locked founding prices forever. Both turn the decision into "now or wait."

## Part 3: Six Things That Make a Community Sticky (Mighty Lens)

### 1. Ask, don't post (Gina Bianchini's core insight)
Mighty's founder says hosts think they need **more content**, but members want **connection**. Mighty's own data says **questions and polls get more comments than any other post type**. Her method uses **"unlocking phrases"**: simple, repeatable questions anyone can answer.
**ONE BILL version:** "What's ONE bill you're tackling this week?" · "Hot or cold: canceling streaming services?" · "What's a money win nobody saw?"

### 2. Monthly themes and weekly rituals
The meditation community ($864K ARR) ran on **monthly challenges plus short "journeys" plus live events**. Wealth Without Wall Street has **"Tuesday Q&A"**. A predictable rhythm turns a membership into a habit.
**ONE BILL version:** A monthly "**Kill-a-Bill Challenge**" (one bill category per month: subscriptions, insurance, phone, utilities) with a weekly live check-in.

### 3. Members create the value
The horror community's **1–2 million member contributions** produced **93% revenue retention**. When members are posting, sharing and helping each other, the community doesn't depend on the founder's energy, and it's harder to leave.

### 4. Results you can count
WealthBuilders measures **accounts opened and dollars invested**. Members stay when they can *see* their progress.
**ONE BILL version:** A **community-wide "Bills Killed" counter**: total dollars saved by all members. Every win adds to it, and it becomes your marketing, too.

### 5. Local and in-person layer
Oiselle's Volée has **local team leaders** organizing runs, so the online community becomes real friendships offline.
**ONE BILL version:** **ONE BILL "Kitchen Table" meetups**, hosted by members in their city, with a simple host kit.

### 6. Automated hospitality
Mighty credits **automations** for retention: welcoming new members, recognizing engaged ones, and **re-engaging quiet ones** before they drift. That's Ritz-Carlton service at scale: no one is invisible.
**ONE BILL version:** A day-1 personal welcome, a day-3 "what's your ONE bill?" question, a day-7 buddy intro, and a nudge if someone goes quiet for 14 days.

## Part 5: Founding Members, the Mighty Networks Twist

Everything in the Circle doc still applies: **tell founders the plan on day one, free for life, recognition, a real say**. The Mighty research adds three ideas:

### 1. Use freemium to make "free founders" feel even fairer
With a **free public tier plus a paid inner tier**, the story becomes simple and generous:
- **Free ONE BILL Community**: open to everyone, which serves your mission of financial awareness for all Americans.
- **ONE BILL Inner Circle (paid)**: live coaching, challenges, Q&As, courses.
- **Founding Members get the Inner Circle free for life.**

This removes most of the "am I using them?" tension. **You're not charging people for access to your founders' knowledge. You're charging for extra programming, while the core community stays free for every American.** That fits ONE BILL's mission.

### 2. The Versed lesson: locked promises earn loyalty
When Versed moved platforms, **almost all founding members followed**, because their founding rate "will never increase." Founders stay loyal when your promise holds through every change. Put "**free for life**" in writing and never revisit it.

### 3. Give founders a job with a title, not just a seat
Oiselle uses **local team leaders**, and Wealth Without Wall Street has a dedicated community lead. Offer founders roles they'd be proud of: **Founding Host** (runs a monthly topic), **City Captain** (local meetups) or **Bill Buddy** (welcomes new members). Roles create ownership, and ownership makes it *their* community too, not something they're giving to you.

### Updated founder pitch line (for the freemium model)
> "ONE BILL will always have a free community for every American who wants to get smarter about money. That's the mission. Later we'll add a paid Inner Circle with deeper coaching and live programs. As a Founding Member, you'll be in the Inner Circle **free, for life**, because you helped build it."

## Part 6: Top Takeaways for ONE BILL (Both Platforms Combined)

1. **Build the audience engine first.** A podcast or weekly show (like Wealth Without Wall Street) feeds the community week after week.
2. **Lead with a free front door** to serve the mission, and **sell the transformation** (a paid tier) to sustain it.
3. **Ask questions daily.** "What's your ONE bill?" is your unlocking phrase.
4. **Make progress visible.** A community-wide "Bills Killed" counter.
5. **Monthly challenge plus weekly ritual.** The Kill-a-Bill Challenge plus a Tuesday live.
6. **Founders: free for life, in writing, with titles and a voice.**
7. **Get local.** Kitchen Table meetups.
